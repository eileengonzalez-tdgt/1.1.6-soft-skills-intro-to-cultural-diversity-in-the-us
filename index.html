Clarify · Languages, Identities and Respectful Words

20 min

The United States is home to many languages, communities, and cultural traditions. No single experience represents everyone.

Explore the Language Map

Read the Newsweek language-map article ↗. It explores languages spoken across US states beyond English and Spanish.

Notice: Which languages appear on the map? Which ones are new to you?
Compare: Do all states have the same language patterns? Why might they differ?
Apply: Why shouldn't a customer service agent assume what language someone speaks?

Vocabulary Flashcards

Select a card to flip it. The front shows the term; the back shows its meaning and an example.

People and Identities

Terms such as Mexican American, Chinese American, African American, Indian American, and Native American refer to different identities or communities. People may describe themselves in different ways. Use the identity someone chooses for themselves and avoid assuming identity from appearance, accent, or name.

Useful language: “People in the US have different backgrounds.” · “Some families speak more than one language at home.” · “I don't want to make assumptions.” · “Could you tell me more about that?”

Say It More Respectfully

Instead of: “Everyone in the US celebrates the same holidays.”

Try: “People in the US celebrate many different holidays.”

Instead of: “You must speak Spanish.”

Try: “Which language would you prefer to use?”

Internalize · Practice Real Conversations

15 min

Work in pairs. Select a scenario to reveal guiding questions and language support. Switch roles after your first attempt.

Scenario A · Meeting Someone New

You meet a new coworker who mentions a family tradition you don't know. Show interest without assuming that everyone in their community has the same experience.

Guiding questions:
  • How can you politely ask about the tradition?
  • How can you show that you're listening?
  • What can you say if you don't understand something?
  • How can you share a tradition from your own community without generalizing?

Useful expressions: “That sounds interesting.” · “Would you mind telling me more?” · “In my family, we usually…” · “Thanks for sharing!”

Scenario B · Helping a Customer

A customer says they are more comfortable using another language and asks whether support is available. You don't know which language they prefer or which services your company offers.

Guiding questions:
  • How do you ask for their preferred language?
  • How can you acknowledge their request respectfully?
  • What can you say without promising an unverified service?
  • How would you explain the next step clearly?

Useful expressions: “I'd be happy to check the available options.” · “Which language would you prefer?” · “Thank you for letting me know.” · “Let me confirm what support is available.”

Fluent Use · Compare, Communicate, Reflect

15 min

Group Discussion

Compare cultural diversity in the United States with cultural diversity in your country. Speak from your own experience and avoid generalizations.

Discussion prompts:
  1. What languages are spoken in your country?
  2. What cultural traditions or celebrations do you know?
  3. What similarities and differences can you identify between your country and the US?
  4. Why is it important to avoid stereotypes?
  5. How can cultural awareness help in a customer service conversation?

Mini Challenge · 60-Second Response

Imagine a customer tells you: “People often make assumptions about me because of my accent.” Respond with empathy, then explain how you would help them feel respected.

Speaking frame: “I'm sorry that happened. Thank you for telling me. I want to make sure…”
